# Office Hours — demand diagnostic

**Mode:** startup, pre-product. **Position:** do not start with “SaaS.” Sell one archive sweep manually and use software only to make the evidence packet repeatable.

## Six forcing questions

1. **Demand reality:** industry DAI adoption and owners discussing stale reads prove the condition exists, not demand for SlotSweep. No payment, deposit, interview, or workflow dependency exists. Strongest test: a qualified owner shares an RSS feed and accepts a paid $400 scope.
2. **Status quo:** the owner ignores old reads, manually re-edits source audio, or uses host-native segment controls with an editor. The measurable cost is blocked back-catalog inventory plus operator hours; it must be calculated per show.
3. **Desperate specificity:** owner/operator of an independent evergreen show with 100+ episodes, at least 1,000 monthly back-catalog downloads, stale sponsor reads, a DAI-capable host, and current advertiser access. “Podcasters” is too broad.
4. **Narrowest paid wedge:** a 10-episode, traffic-ranked evidence-and-execution pilot for $400. No dashboard subscription is required to deliver it.
5. **Observation:** none yet. Watch three owners inspect the report without explanation and perform approval in their real host. Record where confidence fails, which evidence they open, and whether they ask about revenue or brand safety first.
6. **Future-fit:** host-native detection will commoditize the scan. SlotSweep becomes more essential only if it owns cross-host QA, approval history, migration workflow, and trusted operator delivery; otherwise it becomes less essential.

## Premise challenge

- The outcome is recovered, safely verified inventory—not “AI transcript analysis.”
- Traffic ranking matters because cleaning low-listen episodes cannot justify labor.
- Owner approval is mandatory because false positives and destructive edits damage trust.
- Doing nothing remains rational when estimated recovered value is below the service price.

## Approaches considered

### A — Concierge leak report (recommended)
**Effort:** S · **Risk:** Low. Public RSS + transcript scan, evidence packet, human approval. Fastest paid test; deliberately manual host execution. Reuses host-native editing tools.

### B — Self-serve scanner
**Effort:** M · **Risk:** Medium. Owner pastes RSS and receives a prioritized queue. More scalable, but asks for product trust before demand exists and may compete with free host features.

### C — Cross-host archive operations platform
**Effort:** XL · **Risk:** High. Credentials, editing integrations, approvals, QA, audit trail, reseller controls. Ideal long-term architecture, but premature and dangerous without host partnerships.

## Success criteria and assignment

Within 14 days: 20 qualified owner conversations, 5 archive scans accepted, 3 unassisted report observations, and at least 2 explicit $400 pilot acceptances. Kill or reframe if fewer than 5 qualified owners provide an RSS feed, or if estimated inventory value rarely exceeds the fee. The next action is to create three real leak reports from public RSS feeds and ask owners to buy the execution—not join a waitlist.
