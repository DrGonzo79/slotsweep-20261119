# Source notes — 2026-11-19

Derived analysis of the live free [IdeaBrowser Idea of the Day](https://www.ideabrowser.com/hub/ideas/today), read in the authenticated browser on 2026-11-19. This is not a reproduction of the report.

## Report snapshot

- **Title:** SaaS platform that cleans up podcast archives and prepares old episodes for new ads
- **IB Index:** 6.5; B2B SaaS; estimated ceiling **$600K–$1M ARR**
- **Reality:** moderate difficulty; $2K–$10K starting capital; first revenue in 30–60 days
- **Buyer:** independent evergreen podcast owners with meaningful current back-catalog downloads and advertiser access; production agencies are the plausible reseller.
- **Painful status quo:** stale baked-in sponsor reads, dead promo codes, and expired offers keep playing. Owners must locate the segment, edit or mark it in their host, get approval, and QA the feed—or leave the slot unsellable.
- **Wedge:** traffic-prioritized, one-host-at-a-time done-for-you execution with explicit owner approval. The gap is labor and judgment, not missing DAI software.
- **Why now:** the report cites IAB/PwC saying DAI carried 93.6% of US podcast ad revenue in March 2026, $2.9B US spend in 2025 and a $3.0B 2026 track; automated buying has expanded while Whisper/LLM-assisted segment detection made the scan cheap.

## Competition and evidence

Host/network tooling already exists: Captivate AMIE/Ad Painter, Spotify Megaphone Segment Editing, Buzzsprout Dynamic Content, Podbean PodAds, Castos, and Audioboom AdRip. We Edit Podcasts and thousands of Fiverr editors anchor generic editing prices. The defensible claim is therefore **operator workflow + QA + trust**, not proprietary removal technology.

Directional demand signals include podcast-owner discussions about remastering catalogs and DAI income, host blog warnings about stale offers, the reported industry migration to DAI, and live host-migration events as an outreach trigger. Evidence quality is limited: no buyer interview, paid pilot, conversion data, or observed workflow for this exact service is supplied.

Source domains named by the report include IAB/PwC, Spotify Advertising, Magellan AI/DMEXCO, Edison Research, Podnews, Libsyn, Castos, Reddit/r/podcasting, Fiverr, XDA Developers, and GitHub.

## Offer ladder and GTM

1. Free Archive Leak Report: public RSS scan with likely stale reads and sellable-slot estimate.
2. $400 flat 10-episode pilot: rank by 90-day downloads, approve every swap, execute in native host tooling, and QA.
3. $600/month retainer: 20 episodes/month plus quarterly catalog audit.
4. $1,500 migration cleanup.

The report rejects paid search: solution-keyword volume is described as zero and adjacent “podcast editing service” as only 320 searches/month at $17.62 CPC. Proposed motion is 30 personalized weekly outreach messages tied to Podnews migrations and r/podcasting activity. Its model assumes 15% replies, 25% reply-to-pilot conversion, and 60% pilot-to-retainer attachment; none is validated.

## Risks and build implications

- Neglected archives may belong to owners least likely to act or pay.
- Large shows already have editors/networks; small shows may not have enough replay inventory value.
- A host-native one-click scan could erase the scanning SKU.
- Sales is high-touch and founder-fit requires podcast ad-ops credibility.
- Incorrect timestamps or destructive edits create sponsor and audience trust risk.

The prototype therefore tests the **leak report and approval queue**, not audio editing: deterministic transcript heuristics, traffic-ranked candidates, transparent evidence, editable CPM, and a human approval state. Demo data and simulated analysis are explicitly labeled.
